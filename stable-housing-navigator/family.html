Family & Child Support

Family & Children's Housing Needs

Families face unique barriers — and unique protections. This page helps you navigate school stability, childcare, safety, and keeping your family connected to services.

🏫 School Stability

When families face housing instability, children's education is often disrupted — but federal law protects them.

Under the McKinney-Vento Homeless Assistance Act (42 U.S.C. § 11431):

  • Children experiencing homelessness have the right to stay enrolled in their school of origin (the school they were attending before housing loss)
  • Schools must enroll children immediately — even without immunization records, proof of residency, school records, or a guardian's ID
  • Transportation to the school of origin must be provided
  • Children cannot be separated or segregated based on homeless status
  • "Homeless" includes: staying with others (doubled up), shelters, motels, cars, or other inadequate housing

What to do: Contact your school district's McKinney-Vento homeless liaison. Every district must have one. If your child is being denied enrollment or told to wait, the liaison must act.

Next step

Call your school district's main office and ask: "I need to speak with the McKinney-Vento liaison." King County has multiple school districts — Seattle, Bellevue, Kent, Renton, Federal Way, Auburn, and others each have their own liaison.

Source: McKinney-Vento Homeless Assistance Act, 42 U.S.C. § 11431

🧒 Childcare

Childcare can be a critical piece of housing stability — you can't work to pay rent if you can't afford childcare.

  • DSHS Childcare Assistance Program (CCAP): Subsidized childcare for working families and families in job training. Verify eligibility
  • ECEAP (Early Childhood Education & Assistance Program): Preschool and childcare for income-qualified families — a Head Start alternative in Washington State
  • Head Start / Early Head Start: Federal program for children under 5 from low-income families. Contact your local Head Start program
  • Emergency childcare programs: Call 211 to ask about emergency or sliding-scale childcare in your area

Apply for childcare assistance

Call DSHS at 1-844-626-8687 or apply online through WashingtonConnection.org. Note: waitlists may apply — apply as early as possible.

Tip: Some shelters and housing programs include on-site childcare or can help arrange emergency childcare. Ask when contacting any program.

🏠 Finding Safe Housing for Your Family

Families with children have additional housing rights and some priority preferences:

  • Fair Housing Act familial status protection: Landlords generally cannot discriminate against families with children under 18. You cannot be refused housing solely because you have children (with narrow exceptions like senior housing)
  • Bedroom requirements: Many affordable housing programs have minimum bedroom standards. A family should not be required to fit 2+ children in an unsafe single room
  • Larger unit vouchers: If you have a voucher, your bedroom size is determined by family composition. Ensure your housing authority has the correct family size on file
  • Habitability and children: If your current housing has lead paint, mold, pests, or other hazards that affect children, document it and contact the Tenants Union or legal aid

Family housing preference programs

Some housing authorities have preferences for families with children in certain programs. Call KCHA (206-214-1300) or SHA (206-615-3300) and ask about any family preferences for currently open programs. Verify current preferences

🥗 Food, Diapers, and Basic Needs

  • SNAP/Basic Food: Apply through DSHS at 1-877-501-2233 or WashingtonConnection.org. Emergency benefits may be available within 7 days for families in extreme hardship
  • WIC (Women, Infants, Children): Food and nutrition support for pregnant women, new mothers, infants, and children under 5. Call 1-800-841-1410 or contact King County Public Health Verify
  • Diapers: Some food banks and community programs provide diapers. Call 211 and ask specifically about diaper programs
  • School meals: Children in schools may qualify for free or reduced-price meals. Contact your school district's food services
  • Summer food programs: Available in many King County parks and community sites when school is not in session

🛡️ Safety with Children

If you and your children need to leave an unsafe home:

  • You can leave — and you can take your children
  • DV emergency shelters welcome children in most cases — call ahead to confirm
  • Under VAWA, your housing assistance generally cannot be terminated solely because of domestic violence
  • If CPS (Child Protective Services) is involved, contact an attorney or legal aid immediately to understand your rights
  • YWCA and DAWN both serve families with children

DV Crisis with children — call now

DAWN: 425-656-8423 | YWCA: 206-461-4882 | WA DV Hotline: 1-800-562-6025

💳 Benefits for Families

  • TANF (Temporary Assistance for Needy Families): Cash assistance for families with children. Apply through DSHS
  • Medicaid / Apple Health for Kids: Free or low-cost health coverage for children and families. Apply through Washington Healthplanfinder
  • CHIP: Children's Health Insurance Program — for children who don't qualify for Medicaid but can't afford private insurance Verify current WA program
  • SSI for children with disabilities: If your child has a qualifying disability, they may be eligible for SSI income
  • Child support enforcement: DSHS Division of Child Support can help collect support if owed — 1-800-442-KIDS

Apply for family benefits

Apply at WashingtonConnection.org or call DSHS: 1-877-501-2233. A benefits counselor can help you apply for multiple programs at once.

🚌 Transportation with Children

  • ORCA LIFT: Reduced-fare transit for low-income riders. Children under 6 typically ride free on King County Metro Verify current rules
  • School transportation: McKinney-Vento requires school districts to provide transportation to the school of origin for homeless children
  • Medical transport: DSHS clients may qualify for medical transport assistance — ask your caseworker
  • Program-specific transport: Some housing and DV programs can provide transportation assistance. Ask every program you contact

✅ Keeping Your Family Connected After Housing is Obtained

Getting housed is the beginning — these steps help families stay stable after placement:

  • Update your address with your school district to continue receiving services
  • Notify your children's school and update enrollment records
  • Update your address with DSHS for benefits continuity
  • Connect with a case manager or family support program for ongoing support
  • Apply for or continue childcare assistance
  • Set up utility accounts in your name and apply for utility assistance programs
  • Set a reminder to review your lease 60 days before renewal
  • Keep documenting — maintain records of your new lease, utility bills, and income
